Ore Economy

What each way of handling ore earns, from the game's own numbers: merchant prices, recipes, furnace heat and recovery, ore grades, boulder crushing and refining, tool speed and fuel.

Basis: 100 items of mined ore, at the purity a stack comes out of a node with (the ore's mid grade × 0.75, since a node's face is mixed with stone the pick takes at half rate). Prices from Merchant prices.asset; recipes from Assets/Config/Recipes; branch p12/eroded-landscape, 2026-11-03.

Findings

Exploit

Crushing makes metal out of nothing

A crushed item keeps its count of one while half its rock goes, so its mineral share is renormalised upward. 100 chalcopyrite at 16% holds 16 units of copper mineral; crushed, the same 100 items hold 27, plus 42 crushed stone. Crush, then smelt, gives about 1.7× the copper of smelting raw.

Exploit

Crushed stone is worth ten times stone

Crushed stone sells for 1 coin an item, stone for 0.1. Crushing low-grade chalcopyrite returns more coins in crushed stone (about 42) than the raw ore was worth (about 32).

Big lever

Smelting is where the money is

A unit of copper mineral sells for 2 coins as ore and 10.5 to 14 as a bun, depending on the furnace's recovery: 5 to 7 times as much. Gold and silver keep the same 4.5 to 6× ratio.

Balance

Refining adds no sale value

Prices scale straight with purity, so picking the stone out of a stack leaves its value the same. What refining buys is fewer furnace runs, less fuel and fewer inventory slots: for 16% chalcopyrite, 21 seconds at the boulder saves 67 furnace-seconds and about 17 coins of wood.

Balance

Ingots only pay with Blacksmithing

Ingot quality is capped by the smith (50% at level 0, 100% at 10). Below Blacksmithing 7, hammering a silver or gold bun loses money (a gold bun is 900, its level 0 ingot 550). Copper, tin and bronze ingots barely beat buns until the mid levels.

Balance

Bronze breaks even in a stone furnace

Alloying turns 3 copper and 1 tin bun (54 coins) into 4 bronze buns times the furnace's recovery. The stone furnace's 0.75 gives exactly 3 buns, 54 coins, minus fuel. In a clay, brick or refractory furnace (recovery 1) it's 72 coins: a third more.

Scale

Gold dwarfs everything else

100 gold ore smelts to about 11,400 coins in a stone furnace; 100 chalcopyrite to about 170. Gold nodes are half as common and deeper, but one gold stack is worth some 65 copper stacks.

Not the limit

Digging is fast; finding and hauling isn't

A pick takes about 29 (stone), 34 (copper) or 43 (bronze) items' worth of ground per hit at one hit a second, so 100 items of ore is a few seconds of swinging. Finding nodes, walking, stack limits (256 ore) and furnace time decide the rate.

Leaves burn for 2 s and sell 10 for a coin, so they're the cheapest heat by far, if you can keep a furnace fed. Charcoal stretches wood by half (4 wood, 80 s of burn, make 2 charcoal, 120 s) for 14 furnace-seconds of cooking.

How it's worked out

  • Selling ore: price × count × purity (PriceList.ValueOf: anything sells at its quality's share; a gathered stack's quality is its own mineral's share).
  • Refining (Refining.Pass): takes 1 item of impurities per pass, a pass every 0.25 s (BoulderBashing), never the mineral. 100 items at purity p become 100p items at 100%, and 100(1 − p) of stone come back.
  • Crushing (Crushing.Crush): ore minerals stay, half the rest goes; the item count doesn't change, so purity becomes 2p / (1 + p), and (1 − p) / 2 of an item of crushed stone comes back per item. 0.25 s an item. Only cassiterite, chalcopyrite and malachite have crushed forms.
  • Smelting (CraftingStation.YieldOf): a recipe's output count is what pure mineral gives; it yields that × the mineral's share × the furnace's quality (recovery × what it's built of). Work time is the recipe's amount ÷ heat (heat × what it's built of). Smelted metal comes out at 100% quality.
  • Furnaces: campfire heat 0.75, recovery 0.5 (tin only); stone furnace 1, 0.75; clay 1.5, 1; brick 2.25, 1; refractory 3, 1.
  • Ingots: 6 strikes at the anvil (12 on a flat rock); quality = min(bun quality, 0.5 + 0.05 × Blacksmithing) (CraftingStation.SmithingQuality).
  • Mining speed: measured with SphereStrike against a solid face at 0.25 m voxels; the tool cooldown is 1 s (ToolCooldown).
  • Not counted: walking, finding nodes, experience and unlocks, tool wear, fuel loading, and silver traces in copper ores (up to 3%, kept in buns' contents but not paid for).

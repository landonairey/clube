## Tool progression path

- [ ] Hands can only collect dirt, grass, clay, leaves, and loose surface stones. → **GL145**
- [ ] Take a stone and bash it (right click) on a larger surface boulder to make a Hand Axe tool item from 1 stone. → **GL146**
- [ ] The Hand Axe can collect wood and hard stone and ore materials; it's just not efficient at it. → **GL146**
- [ ] The pointy stick is removed from the game. → **GL147**
- [ ] Making a Hand Axe grants a small amount of Crafting xp. → **GL146**
- [ ] The next major unlock is crafting the stone pick and stone axe we already have, at the tree stump. → (already in place: Crafting 2 at the stump)
- [ ] A later Mining unlock: use the boulder to pulverize ore, which makes crushed stone as a byproduct and crushed ore as the valuable output. Crushed ore still has some stone content, but half of what it had before. Crushed ore is more valuable than raw ore and gives a better yield when smelted. → **GL148**

## Changes

- [ ] Just like the loose stones on the terrain surface, add some loose stones within the dirt layer. Both can be hand collected as long as they're only 1-3 connected voxels of stone surrounded by dirt, grass or clay. If one is surrounded by cassiterite, the player can't hand collect it. → **GL149**
- [ ] Add a new tree type, so there are birch trees and maple trees. Maple wood is more valuable than birch wood. → **GL150**
- [ ] Maple sticks make tools with higher durability than birch sticks. → **GL151**
- [ ] A higher Woodcutting level is needed to cut down maple trees. Birch can be cut with a hand axe from the start. A bronze axe is needed to cut down a maple tree. → **GL152**
